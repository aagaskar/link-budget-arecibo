Model notes & assumptions

  • Physics follows the Python notebook in notebook/: Shannon capacity in the power-limited limit, C = (P/N₀)/ln 2, with free-space path loss and k·T receiver noise. The “Notebook baseline” preset reproduces its result (~1.1 kbps for Arecibo ↔ Arecibo over 10 ly at 450 kW).
  • Antenna pattern: uniformly illuminated circular aperture (Airy pattern), G(θ)/G₀ = [2·J₁(x)/x]², x = (πD/λ)·sin θ — exact main lobe, optimistic sidelobes, floored at −60 dB. Real tapered feeds have a slightly wider main lobe and lower sidelobes.
  • Host-star noise is modeled with Planck’s law exactly as in the notebook and shown in the ledger — it lands ~70+ dB below receiver thermal noise, i.e. negligible, which the notebook proves at length.
  • Aim-ahead: stars move. The transmitter must point where the Sun will be one light-travel-time from now: an offset of μ·(R/c). For Tau Ceti (μ ≈ 1.9″/yr, 11.9 ly) that is ~23″ — a quarter of the 305 m dish’s beamwidth — so “point at the star you see” is already a pointing error.
  • Not modeled: interstellar scintillation and dispersion, atmospheric effects, polarization mismatch, modem implementation loss (Shannon capacity is an upper bound no real coder reaches), and the transmitter’s ability to know where Earth is to sub-beamwidth accuracy in the first place.
  • The book’s answer: the notebook concludes a meaningful radio link home was “not quite possible” — capacity scales with D², and nobody packs a 305 m dish on a ship. The novel agrees: the Hail Mary sends its science home as physical storage on the beetle probes. Crank the transmit dish below ~30 m here and watch the verdict chip agree.
